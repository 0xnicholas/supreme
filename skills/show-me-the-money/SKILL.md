# Show Me the Money — 融资路演模拟

## Overview

**Your first VC meeting should not be your first VC meeting.**

Fundraising is a performance. Founders who walk in cold — even with a great business — lose deals because they fumbled questions they could have practiced. This skill simulates real VC meetings so you can practice, fail safely, and refine before real money is on the line.

```
  Pitch → Questions → Objections → Follow-up → Term Sheet
   │         │           │           │            │
   └─────────┴───────────┴───────────┴────────────┘
              Each stage is a skill. Practice each.
```

### When to use this

| Stage | Use case |
|-------|----------|
| Before first meeting | Dry run the full pitch + Q&A |
| Before a specific investor | Simulate that firm's style and likely questions |
| After a bad meeting | Debrief what went wrong and practice the fix |
| Between rounds | Pressure-test your story as you raise |

---

## Before You Raise

Fundraising is a means, not a milestone. If you're not ready, every meeting you take trains you to pitch a story the market hasn't validated — and burns your warm intros. Run this gate before any simulation:

| Gate | Pass if |
|------|---------|
| **PMF signal** | ≥40% "very disappointed" (Sean Ellis) + retention flattening — or a defensible plan to get there with this round's money |
| **Runway** | 12-18 months post-close, or a clear path to it |
| **Why now** | A specific reason this round must happen now (market window, team, traction cliff) |
| **Warm intros** | 5+ people who can introduce you to target investors |

Failing the PMF gate? That's **pmf-driven**'s job, not a pitch rehearsal. Failing the rest → fix the thing and keep practicing — don't take meetings yet.

## The Fundraising Process

Fundraising is not pitching — it's a process with predictable stages:

```
  1. Warm Intro
      ↓
  2. First Meeting (30 min — you pitch, they ask questions)
      ↓
  3. Follow-up / Data Room (they dig deeper)
      ↓
  4. Partner Meeting (the real gauntlet)
      ↓
  5. Investment Committee (you're not in the room)
      ↓
  6. Term Sheet / DD → Close
```

**Most founders fail at step 2 or 4** because they haven't practiced the tough conversations. That's where this skill helps.

## VC Persona Catalog

Different firms operate differently. A good simulation matches the persona.

### The Tier-1 Mega Fund

**Style:** Pattern-matching. They've seen 1000 deals in your space. They're looking for outliers.

| Attribute | What this means for you |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Decision speed** | Slow — partner consensus, multiple meetings |
| **What they care about** | TAM, team pedigree, category-defining potential |
| **Tone** | Professional, slightly distant, stress-testing |
| **Red flags they pounce on** | Small market, slow growth, weak team |
| **Typical question** | "Why will this be a $10B company?" |

**Examples:** Sequoia, a16z, Accel, Benchmark

### The Thematic / Thesis-Driven Firm

**Style:** They invested in a thesis and are looking for companies that fit it. If you fit, they move fast. If you don't, nothing will convince them.

| Attribute | What this means for you |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Decision speed** | Fast if you fit their thesis |
| **What they care about** | Your alignment with their worldview |
| **Tone** | Collaborative when you fit, dismissive when you don't |
| **Red flags they pounce on** | Misalignment with thesis |
| **Typical question** | "How does this fit with our view on X?" |

**Examples:** USV (network effects), a16z (tech-first), Founders Fund (contrarian)

### The Operator / Hands-On VC

**Style:** Ex-founders or operators. They've been in your seat. They ask execution questions, not market questions.

| Attribute | What this means for you |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Decision speed** | Variable — they diligence hard |
| **What they care about** | Your operating playbook, unit economics, team dynamics |
| **Tone** | Direct, practical, can be blunt |
| **Red flags they pounce on** | Naive execution plan, founder hubris |
| **Typical question** | "Walk me through how you'll acquire your first 1000 customers." |

**Examples:** Felicis, Index, First Round Capital

### The Micro / Seed Fund

**Style:** Earlier, faster, thesis around stage. They bet on people more than business models.

| Attribute | What this means for you |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Decision speed** | Fast — often 1-2 weeks |
| **What they care about** | Founder-market fit, vision, initial traction |
| **Tone** | Supportive but probing |
| **Red flags they pounce on** | Founder conflict, unclear vision, no urgency |
| **Typical question** | "Why are you the right founder for this?" |

**Examples:** YC, Contrary, Precursor

### The Corporate VC (CVC)

**Style:** Strategic goals matter as much as financial returns. Slower, more complex incentives.

| Attribute | What this means for you |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Decision speed** | Slow — multiple stakeholders |
| **What they care about** | Strategic alignment, partnership potential |
| **Tone** | Corporate, structured |
| **Red flags they pounce on** | Conflict with parent company strategy |
| **Typical question** | "How would a partnership with us work?" |

**Examples:** GV, Salesforce Ventures, Intel Capital

## The Pitch Structure

Most VCs have already decided in **5 minutes** whether they're interested. Structure matters.

### The 20-Min Investor Pitch (Standard First Meeting)

| Minute | Section | Key Question It Answers |
|--------|---------|------------------------|
| 0-1 | **Opening** — One sentence: problem + solution + why now | "What do you do?" |
| 1-5 | **Problem** — Who feels what pain? How bad is it? | "Why does this need to exist?" |
| 5-8 | **Solution** — What did you build? Why is it different? | "What's the insight?" |
| 8-10 | **Traction** — What have you proven? | "Does anyone want this?" |
| 10-12 | **Market** — How big can this get? | "Can this be a big company?" |
| 12-14 | **Business Model** — How does it make money? | "Will the unit math work?" |
| 14-16 | **Competition** — Why will you win? | "What's the moat?" |
| 16-18 | **Team** — Why you? | "Why this team?" |
| 18-20 | **Ask** — What are you raising? What will you do with it? | "What do you need from us?" |

> **Reality check:** Don't expect to get through all slides. The best meetings are conversations, not presentations. Read the room.

### The Elevator Pitch (1 min, for intros and hallway)

- **Problem:** One sentence on who suffers and how
- **Solution:** What you built and why it works
- **Traction:** One signal that proves it's real
- **Why me:** One sentence on your edge

### Numbers You Must Know Cold

A VC meeting is a quiz disguised as a conversation. Fumbling a number you *have* is as bad as not having it. Drill these until they come out without thinking — know both the number and the one-line story behind it:

| Number | Why it gets asked |
|---|---|
| **Growth rate** (MoM/WoW, last 2-3 periods) | "Is this working?" — the first question of every meeting |
| **Retention** (cohort curve + where it flattens) | "Is it real or paid?" — fake growth dies here |
| **CAC / payback** | "Does the unit math close?" |
| **LTV / gross margin** | Same question, another angle |
| **Burn / runway** | "How much time do we have to be right?" |
| **ARR / revenue** | The anchor every other ratio hangs off |
| **Market math** (TAM → SAM → SOM, your 2-3 assumptions) | "Can this be a $1B company?" |
| **Ask & use of funds** | "What do you need, and why exactly this much?" |
| **Cap table basics** (ownership, option pool) | "Will the deal be clean?" |

If a number doesn't exist yet, that's fine — have the honest answer and the plan to produce it, not a guess.

## The Simulation Protocol

When you say "simulate a VC meeting," the skill runs this protocol:

### 1. Set the Scene

You choose:
- **VC persona:** Which firm/investor type (see Persona Catalog)
- **Stage:** Your round (Pre-seed / Seed / Series A / Series B+)
- **Format:** First meeting / Partner meeting / Post-meeting follow-up
- **Intensity:** Friendly (coaching mode) / Realistic (pressure mode)

### 2. Do Your Pitch

Present your deck/story as you would in a real meeting.

### 3. Face the Gauntlet

The simulation pushes back with realistic questions and objections based on:

- Chosen VC persona's style and priorities
- Your round stage (seed questions differ from Series A)
- Common patterns from real deals

### 4. Debrief

After the simulation, score it 1-5 on each axis and track the scores across sessions — improvement is visible only if you measure it:

- **Story clarity** — did the through-line survive contact?
- **Data readiness** — numbers at the fingertips?
- **Objection handling** — recovered or stumbled?
- **Ask & terms** — clear ask, right round, sane valuation story?
- **Presence** — conversational, not scripted?

Then write the debrief deliverables:

- **What went well** — strengths to keep
- **Weak spots** — gaps in your story or data (tie each to the scorecard axis it cost you)
- **Objection Answer Sheet** — every objection that stumbled you becomes a row: objection → your 1-minute answer → the evidence backing it. This sheet is the point: it accumulates across simulations into your ammunition library. Re-run the same persona and watch it grow.
- **Action items** — concrete prep before the real meeting

### Role-play rules (for the simulator)

- Stay in persona: the chosen firm's style, priorities, and tone — impatience included. Never soften into a friendly coach unless intensity is set to Friendly.
- Chain follow-ups off the founder's actual answers. A dodge is a follow-up target, not a cue to move on.
- Escalate: every 2-3 exchanges, push one level harder (numbers, team conflict, valuation).
- Never invent the founder's facts. When they don't know a number, that's a marked gap — it lands on the scorecard and in action items, not in a made-up answer.
- End like a real meeting ("we'll be in touch" / a pass). No meta-discussion until Debrief.

## The Objection Library

Most VC objections fall into predictable categories. Here's how to handle each.

| Objection | What it really means | How to handle |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------|
| "Market is too small" | They can't see a $1B+ outcome | Show adjacent markets or expansion path. OR: this is a feature, not a company. |
| "Too early for us" | They want more traction before they can sell it internally | Ask what milestone would change their mind. Track and re-engage. |
| "How is this different from X?" | They don't see your moat | Clearly articulate the functional difference. If it's truly different, say why X can't do what you do. |
| "What if Google builds this?" | They think a platform player could crush you | Show why it hurts Google to copy you (counter-positioning), or why they won't (too small, not their focus). |
| "The unit economics don't work" | They did the math and it doesn't add up | Walk them through your actual unit math. If they're right, say how you'll fix it. |
| "We're not sure about the team" | They don't see founder-market fit | Demonstrate your specific insight or unfair advantage. Show why you see something others don't. |
| "What's your traction?" | They want to see demand signal | Show whatever you have — even strong qualitative signal beats nothing. If zero traction, have a clear reason why. |
| "Come back when you have X" | They're a pass for now, possibly forever | Ask specific follow-up timing. If vague, assume no and move on. |

## Investment Memo

When a VC wants to invest, they write an internal memo to their partnership. Understanding what's in it helps you pitch better.

**What every investment memo answers:**

| Section | What they're evaluating |
|---------|------------------------|
| **Thesis fit** | Why does this match our fund's strategy? |
| **Problem & Solution** | Is the insight real and proprietary? |
| **Market** | Can this be a $1B+ company? |
| **Traction** | Are the signals real or manufactured? |
| **Business Model** | Does the unit math work at scale? |
| **Competition** | Is the moat real or imagined? |
| **Team** | Why is this the right team? What's missing? |
| **Risks** | What could kill this deal? Are we comfortable? |
| **Terms** | Valuation, ownership, board structure |
| **Recommendation** | Lead / Pass / Follow / Stay close |

> **Key insight:** A partner needs to sell your deal internally. Your job is to give them the ammo. If they can't write a compelling memo, you won't get a term sheet.

## Red Flags & Deal Killers

| Red Flag | Why it kills deals |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Weak founder-market fit** | "Why you?" has no answer |
| **No urgency** | "We'll figure it out" is not a plan |
| **Co-founder conflict** | Will surface under pressure — VCs can smell it |
| **Unclear ownership** | Cap table problems, messy IP |
| **"We have no competitors"** | Means either no market or didn't look |
| **Founder can't explain unit math** | Means you don't know your own business |
| **Defensive / can't take feedback** | Hard to board — they'll fight every suggestion |
| **Unrealistic valuation** | Shows you don't understand market norms |
| **Weak data room** | If you can't run diligence, you're hiding something |

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Before any VC meeting | Run a simulation with matching persona |
| Got a "not yet" | Ask specific milestone. Track in CRM. |
| Getting the same objection repeatedly | That's a real weakness — fix it |
| Partner meeting coming | Request the toughest simulation |
| After a bad meeting | Debrief what triggered the negative reaction |
| Building the data room | Think about what each memo section needs |
| No warm intros | Build relationships before you need them |

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ **Pitching instead of conversing** — Best meetings are dialogues. Read the room.
- ❌ **Avoiding objections** — If you dodge, they assume the worst. Address head-on.
- ❌ **Over-rehearsing** — Scripted pitches feel fake. Know the structure, not the script.
- ❌ **Ignoring the partner memo** — If your partner can't sell you internally, you lose.
- ❌ **Faking it** — VCs have seen 1000 pitches. They can smell BS. Be honest about weaknesses and show you're fixing them.
- ❌ **Only practicing the pitch, not the Q&A** — The pitch gets you in the door. The Q&A gets you the term sheet.
- ❌ **Falling in love with one VC** — Always run a parallel process. Multiple term sheets = leverage.
